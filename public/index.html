There is no framework in this app. No bundle, no runtime, no rewrite. It is a WebView that has been told exactly what to refuse to do — and then it gets out of the way.

01 — The problem

Four things your phone is doing
that you never asked for

01

It reports in

Crash dumps, session replays, funnel analytics, a font CDN. Roughly fifty-three hosts you never installed, all phoning home while you type.

02

It blurs everything

Every tooltip and popover renders a live blur of whatever sits behind it. On a weak GPU that is the most expensive thing on screen, and it runs constantly.

03

It reloads when you turn your phone

Rotate the device and the WebView is destroyed and rebuilt. A white screen and a full re-download. Every single time.

04

It dies instead of recovering

When the renderer runs out of memory, stock WebView takes the whole app down with it. On a two-gigabyte phone that is not an edge case.

02 — Mechanism

Six changes.
That is the entire product.

01

Requests die at the socket

Fifty-three tracker hosts and four Discord-side endpoints are matched in shouldInterceptRequest — before DNS, before TLS, before a byte of payload. Blocking after the fact still costs you the radio wake-up. Blocking here costs nothing.

A blocklist, never an allowlist. A host we failed to anticipate degrades to loading normally, never to breaking.

02

Blur is stripped from the paint tree

A blurred backdrop allocates a render target and re-runs a GPU blur over everything behind it, every frame. One CSS rule removes it globally. Popovers keep their own solid background, so nothing shifts.

The single most expensive thing on a low-end screen, deleted in one line.

03

Animation collapses to 0.01 ms

Not none — that would break every modal listening for animationend. At 0.01 ms the motion is gone but the events still fire, so menus, popovers and dialogs keep working. It is the same trick the OS uses for reduced motion.

Looks instant. Costs nothing. Breaks nothing.

04

Images stop being absurd

Avatars cap at 96 px instead of 1024, re-encode as WebP, and drop their srcset so the phone stops paying for a retina copy it cannot use.

Signed ex / is / hm parameters are rewritten byte-for-byte, so images still resolve.

05

Rotating the phone changes nothing

A configChanges declaration covering orientation, density, font scale and keyboard state stops Android from destroying the Activity. You turn the device; the chat stays exactly where it was.

Saves a white screen and a full re-download, every time.

06

A crash is a restart, not an exit

When the renderer runs out of memory, LiteCord tears down the dead WebView, builds a fresh one and reloads. You might lose a few seconds on a busy channel. You do not lose the app.

The difference between an annoyance and a brick.

    05 — Completeness

    It is still Discord

    Nothing was cut from the features. Things were cut from the cost of running them.

    • Servers, channels, threads, DMs, group DMs
    • Voice and video calls, screen share
    • File and image upload
    • Emoji, reactions, markdown
    • Nitro, account switching, two-factor
    • Push notifications
    • Works offline once loaded, caches like a browser

    One deliberate trade: tapping an image opens the downscaled version, not the original. If you need full resolution, open it in your browser.

    06 — Questions

    Reasonable ones

    Is this made by Discord?

    No. LiteCord is an independent, unofficial project — not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Discord Inc. It contains no Discord code. It loads discord.com the same way your browser does.

    Am I logging in through a proxy? Will I get banned?

    You are logging in on discord.com, in a WebView, the same way you would in Chrome. LiteCord has no account system, no server, and no API of its own — it makes no network requests to anyone except Discord and the image CDN. Your credentials never touch anything we control.

    What permissions does it ask for?

    Internet, microphone, camera. That is the complete list. Mic and camera are requested only when you actually join a call, and the app refuses to grant the page anything you declined.

    how is a 533 KB app able to do all this?

    Because it is not reimplementing Discord. It is a browser pointed at discord.com with a filter in front of it. Almost all of the size is the Android runtime; the optimisation layer itself is about 9 KB of CSS and JavaScript.

    Which phones?

    Android 8.0 and up. The renderer-crash recovery that keeps a low-RAM phone alive requires Android 8.0, so we did not build a version for anything older — it would have been the version that crashes.

    Is it on the Play Store?

    Not yet. It is a signed APK you sideload. Once installed it behaves like a normal app, updates included.
